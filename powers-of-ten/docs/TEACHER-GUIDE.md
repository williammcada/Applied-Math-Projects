# Powers of Ten — Teacher guide

**A WILLIAM MCADA PRODUCT** · App 0.1.0 · Content 1.0.0

## Prepare and teach

Plan two 45-minute lessons after teaching signed powers, scientific notation and metric conversion. Pairs alternate Planner/Checker and building responsibilities. Supply millimeter rulers, paper/card, scissors, tape, and modeling material for a cell disc. A 600 mm workspace or ruler/tape is needed for the Earth–Moon track. Arrange another pair's model or a teacher example for the gallery comparison. Timing and device suitability require classroom confirmation.

Day 1: introduce the museum commission; complete notation, units and the eight operations; choose a track and check the scale plan. Print a build sheet and calibrated guides, export progress, and begin construction if time permits. Day 2: construct, measure and check deviations; repair where needed; add a source-supported placard and interpretation; visit the other track; collect each learner's separate transfer response; inspect/review and print the report.

The `?` beside each checkpoint gives vocabulary, method, an unrelated worked example and an error-prevention prompt. Help and revision do not incur penalties. The calculator performs one explicit operation; it does not evaluate arbitrary typed code. Oral answers may be transcribed. Paper/oral transfers need a receipt record and teacher review. Keep the learner's actual response as the evidence.

## Answers and accepted reasoning

Use the supplied rounded science values consistently. Calculations are exact on these teaching values; no hidden significant-figure tolerance is applied. Scientific finals require a coefficient from 1 inclusive to 10 exclusive. C05a also accepts 8 with a blank exponent. Ordinary-notation prompts require ordinary decimals. Common-power rewrites may use any allowed exponent if each rewritten operand is equivalent.

| C02 | DNA < specimen depth < cell; 8 × 10⁻⁵ m is ten times the cell diameter, one decade larger |
| C03a | 8 µm = 0.000008 m = 0.008 mm = 8000 nm |
| C03b | 1.28 × 10⁹ cm = 1.28 × 10⁷ m |
| C04a | Raw 20 × 10⁻⁵; final 2 × 10⁻⁴ m |
| C04b | Raw/final 3.84 × 10⁸ m |
| C05a | Raw 0.8 × 10¹; final 8 diameter-widths, dimensionless |
| C05b | Raw 0.5 × 10³; final 5 × 10² s |
| C06a | Example (24 + 7.5) × 10⁻⁶; final 3.15 × 10⁻⁵ m |
| C06b | Example (38.4 + 6) × 10⁷; final 4.44 × 10⁸ m |
| C07a | (8 − 2) × 10⁻⁶ = 6 × 10⁻⁶ m |

Zero is not an acceptable approximation for tiny nonzero values. Ratio fields accept positive equivalent ratios in the stated model:actual direction. Fractions are accepted in scale-factor fields. Length conversion is separate from rounding a construction target.

## Physical models

| Quantity | Track A: cell enlargement | Track B: Earth–Moon reduction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale factor | 0.16 m ÷ 0.000008 m = 20,000 | 0.02 m ÷ 12,800,000 m = 1/640,000,000 |
| Model:actual | 20,000:1 | 1:640,000,000 |
| Construction | Cell diameter 160 ±2 mm | Earth 20 ±1 mm; Moon 5.5 ±1 mm; centers 600 ±5 mm |
| Exact Moon result | Not applicable | 5.46875 mm, rounded once to 5.5 mm for construction |
| Optional comparison | 1.7 m person → 34,000 m; 2 nm DNA width → 0.04 mm | Sun diameter → 2.1875 m; Earth–Sun distance → 234.375 m |

For the cell, make an intact biconcave disc with a shallow depression, not a hole or nucleus. Diameter alone is the scale claim; the activity does not prescribe an exact scaled thickness. For Earth–Moon, measure center to center, not edge to edge. Circle edges may extend beyond strip endpoints. Three measured 200 mm segments make 600 mm; joining tabs do not add distance.

Print at actual size with browser headers/footers off. Check every template's 50 mm calibration line with a ruler; accept within 0.5 mm as the proposed classroom print check. If the line fails, correct printer scaling or construct directly with a ruler. PDF geometry is not proof of printer accuracy. Measure the assembled strip again.

Students enter actual dimensions, units, measuring tool and resolution. Deviation is measured minus target in mm. Tolerance endpoints are inclusive: 158–162 mm for the cell; 19–21, 4.5–6.5 and 595–605 mm for Earth, Moon and centers. Correct arithmetic describing a poor model is accepted as arithmetic and leads to repair. Never type ideal values as if observed.

Use **Record a rebuild** after changing the physical construction. Recheck measurements and repeat inspection. Changing upstream answers makes dependent checks/reviews stale; original evidence is retained.

## Gallery and individual evidence

The available display ratio is 3 m ÷ 2 mm = 1500. Earth diameter/cell diameter is 1.6 × 10¹². A common linear scale is possible mathematically but impractical within those display/visibility limits.

Interpretations should identify what the selected model preserves, what it omits, and a correct numerical limitation. Require a meaningful reference to the supplied source and the correct property. DNA describes width; cell/Earth/Moon/Sun describe diameter; the two orbital comparisons describe distance; light describes speed. All embedded pictures are authored explanatory diagrams, not new observations. The 8 µm cell is a teaching exemplar; distances are rounded/mean values.

The cell placard uses source R2 and diameter. The Earth–Moon placard uses R3/R4 and diameters plus center distance. Source URLs and teaching values appear in the app's reference cards and printable teacher reference, and in the approved specification. No external page is required during core work.

Collect responses independently. The app retains first checked and corrected responses separately. Successful pair work or a corrected answer is not proof of independent mastery. For paper/oral mode, record receipt and review the actual response rather than treating the receipt as correct work.

## Reviews and outcomes

The teacher desk records reviewer alias, role, status, note, time and the exact evidence reviewed. A peer may inspect the physical model; interpretation and paper/oral transfer reviews require a teacher record. Notes should name the inspected dimensions/units and any mismatch, or the reviewed mathematical/scientific reasoning. A local selector cannot authenticate a reviewer.

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft — finish checks | Required current work, build confirmation or transfer submission is missing |
| Provisional — teacher review | An active academic bypass exists; the bypass reason remains in history |
| Calibration required | Reported model fails tolerance, labels need correction, or review requests repair |
| Exhibit ready for review | Current work is complete but a required human review remains pending |
| Featured exhibit | Current required checks, model fit, correct labels, accepted inspection/interpretation and required transfer review are present |

Review conditions describe recorded evidence, not a verified physical fact. Bypass is for a faulty/inaccessible checkpoint with a named reviewer and reason. A later genuine recheck can replace the active bypass while preserving its history. There is no automatic grade. Optional teacher rubric: mathematics 8, representations 4, evidence/interpretation 4, usable documentation 4.

## Preserve work

Export at lesson boundaries and before deletion, browser cleanup or device changes. Import a JSON progress file or bundle through Sessions; copies are the default. Replacing matching saved IDs requires explicit confirmation. Delete removes a complete session; Clear removes only Powers of Ten sessions. Cancel preserves them, and a backup action is offered. No other application's storage is cleared.

When saving fails or another tab has newer work, keep the page open, export current progress, and resolve the conflict through a saved copy. The application does not silently claim a failed save succeeded. Teacher pages contain answers: distribute student print modes separately.
